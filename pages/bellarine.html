AI use policy: the 6 rules

  1. Only approved, business-tier AI tools may be used for work tasks.
  2. Never enter patient or staff personal information into public AI tools.
  3. AI must not release, interpret or change a pathology result. A scientist signs off every result.
  4. AI-generated content sent to patients or GPs must be reviewed by staff.
  5. Any new AI tool needs a documented risk assessment and Operations Manager approval before use.
  6. AI errors or incidents must be reported to the Operations Manager.

Current technology and processes for section 3

Processes

  • Patient and GP enquiries are answered by phone and email by whoever is free
  • Routine slides are screened by eye by scientists

Words look up when stuck
  • Fixed ruleA person wrote the steps. Same input, same result, every time. Not AI. Example: a thermostat.
  • Artificial intelligence (AI)Computers doing jobs that normally need a person to think. Simple test for everyday tools: did it learn from data?
  • Machine learning (ML)A kind of AI that learns from lots of examples instead of following steps a person wrote. Example: a junk-mail filter.
  • Deep learning (DL)A powerful kind of machine learning for language, pictures and sound. Example: face unlock.
  • Large language model (LLM)Deep learning that writes text, like ChatGPT or Copilot. It can sound sure and still be wrong.
  • PolicyThe organisation's rules that staff must follow, like Bellarine's AI use policy above.

More words for Sessions 2 and 3

  • Expert systemAI built from many rules written by experts. It does not learn, but it still counts as AI.
  • Business-tier toolA paid, organisation-managed version of a tool, with privacy and data protections.
